# Too much paper

Barb Fell can turn a ewe and tell from the sound of the flock whether something is wrong in the far corner of Big Field. What Barb cannot do is keep up with the post.

When the online shop opened, a good review in a knitting forum doubled the orders overnight. Customers asked where their parcels were. Suppliers sent new price lists, carriers new rules, the co-op a new form for everything. None of it was hard. All of it was text: emails, attachments, notes on orders, a support inbox, a wiki that Pat in the shop had started and that everyone now edited and nobody owned, and supplier logins in a notebook in the kitchen drawer.

## The kitchen table

By the end of one long autumn, Barb was answering emails at midnight and approving invoices on a phone in the lambing shed. A reorder went in late and the feed ran short. A customer waited a week for a one-minute reply. The staff were good, but few, and each already did three jobs.

It was Pat who said it, at the kitchen table: other businesses had an AI assistant now. It could read the inbox, answer the routine questions, look things up in the wiki and chase the suppliers, and it would not mind the midnight emails.

Barb had learned that anything which promises to save you work usually finds you new work. But the post kept coming, and the argument was never really about whether. It was about how much.

They agreed to start small: answer questions, read documents, and help, but ask before doing anything that mattered.

Everyone at the table believed that was a clear line.
