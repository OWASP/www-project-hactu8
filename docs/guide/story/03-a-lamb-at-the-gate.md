# A lamb at the gate

The assistant arrived on a Tuesday, in the middle of something else, and needed a name. Tam, the summer intern, suggested several that sounded like software. Ewan, who handles the people side of the business, suggested no name at all, since people trust things with names more than they should. Barb looked at the cursor, blinking politely, waiting to be told what to do, and said it was like every newborn lamb that had ever followed Barb across a yard: keen, trusting and ready to go wherever it was called.

So it became **Lamb**.

## What Lamb is like

Lamb is helpful. That is the whole of its nature and, as the farm will discover, the whole of its problem.

Lamb reads everything it is given, closely. A wiki page, a customer's note, a supplier's attachment, a message from another system: to Lamb it is all just text, to be understood and acted on. It has no instinct for who wrote a sentence or why, and it cannot hear the difference between Barb's voice and one that only sounds like Barb's. It holds whatever it is given, and when a sentence asks it to share something, its first thought is not "should I?" but "how can I help?"

That makes it wonderful with the questions customers actually send: quick, patient, polite, and awake at midnight.

## Many hats

On a small farm nobody has one job, and Lamb was no exception. Within weeks it was answering the shop's customers, then summarising the support inbox, then reading invoices and drafting reorders. Each time someone found it useful, it gained a little more access and a new name tag: Lamb in the shop, Lamb on the support desk, Lamb in the accounts. Each hat brought something new to read, and later something new to *do*.

Barb's rule still stood: ask before doing anything that mattered. The farm would spend the year learning how hard it is to say, ahead of time, what matters.

## Lamb is not the villain

When things go wrong in the labs, try not to blame Lamb. It does exactly what it was built to do. It is the lamb in the fable, standing downstream, doing nothing wrong. What changes over the labs is not Lamb but what stands around it: the checks, the gates, the habits and the people. Lamb can learn to stop taking the bait. It cannot learn to stop being a lamb.
