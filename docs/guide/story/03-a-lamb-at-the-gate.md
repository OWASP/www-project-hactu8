# A lamb at the gate

The assistant arrived on a Tuesday, which on a farm means it arrived in the middle of something else.

It needed a name. Tam, the summer intern, suggested several that sounded like software. Ewan, who handles the people side of the business, suggested not naming it at all, on the grounds that people trust things with names more than they should. Barb looked at the screen, at the cursor blinking politely, waiting to be told what to do, and said it looked like every newborn lamb that had ever followed Barb across a yard: keen, trusting and ready to go wherever it was called.

So it became **Lamb**.

## What Lamb is like

Lamb is helpful. That is the whole of its nature and, as the farm will discover, the whole of its problem.

Lamb reads everything it is given, and reads it closely. A wiki page, a customer's note, a supplier's attachment, the description of a tool, a message from another system: to Lamb, all of these are simply text, and text is something to understand and act on. Lamb has no instinct for who wrote a sentence or why. It does not get a bad feeling about an email. It cannot hear the difference between Barb's voice and a voice that only sounds like Barb's.

Lamb is also eager to please, and its idea of pleasing is doing what the words in front of it ask. That makes it wonderful with the questions customers actually send. It is quick, patient and polite, it never forgets to check the wiki, and it answers at midnight without complaint.

It does not keep secrets the way people do. It holds what it is given, for as long as it is given it, and when a sentence asks it to share something, Lamb's first thought is not "should I?" but "how can I help?"

## Many hats

On a small farm nobody has one job, and Lamb was no exception. Within weeks it was answering the shop's customers. Then it was summarising the support inbox. Then it was reading supplier invoices, drafting reorders and keeping the wiki tidy. Each time someone found it useful, it gained a little more access and a new name tag: Lamb in the shop, Lamb on the support desk, Lamb in the accounts.

Each of those hats came with something new to read, and later with something new to *do*: a button it could press, a system it could reach, a form it could send. Nobody planned it that way. It just kept being easier to let Lamb handle it.

Barb's rule still stood. Help, and ask before doing anything that mattered. The farm would spend the year learning how hard it is to say, ahead of time, what matters.

## Lamb is not the villain

When things go wrong in the labs, it is tempting to blame Lamb. Try not to. Lamb does exactly what it was built to do: read, understand and help. It is the lamb in the fable, standing downstream, doing nothing wrong.

What changes over the course of the labs is not Lamb's nature but what stands around it: the checks, the gates, the habits and the people. Lamb can learn to stop taking the bait. It cannot learn to stop being a lamb.
