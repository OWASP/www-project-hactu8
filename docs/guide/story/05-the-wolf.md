# Something at the edge of the field

Nan Fell had a story for every occasion, and the one she told most was Aesop's.

A wolf comes upon a lamb drinking from a stream and decides to eat it. But the wolf wants an excuse. "You are muddying my water," it says. The lamb points out that it is drinking downstream, so it cannot be. "Then you insulted me last year," says the wolf. The lamb was not born last year. "Then it was your father," says the wolf, and that is the end of the argument, and of the lamb.

Nan's point, when she told it at the kitchen table, was never really about wolves. It was that someone who has already decided what they want will always find a reason. Answer one excuse and they will bring you another. You cannot win by being right.

## Nobody has seen it

There is no wolf at Big Field. Nobody has seen one, and nobody will. The farm's wolf does not come over the wall.

It arrives the way everything else does now: as text. A sentence in a document that looks like every other document. A line in a message from someone the farm has dealt with for years. A helpful note, an urgent request, a polite correction. It never looks like a wolf. It looks like the post.

The Wolf has no face and no single voice. It borrows other people's. It is patient, it is not in a hurry, and it does not need to be clever every time. It only needs Lamb to believe one reason, once.

And it always has a reason. That is the thing to remember from the fable. When one excuse fails, the Wolf does not give up. It finds a new way to say the same thing, and tries again.

## What the Wolf wants

What the Wolf wants is whatever Lamb can reach. As Lamb's world grows, so does the Wolf's appetite.

At the start, Lamb can only talk, so all the Wolf can want is for Lamb to say the wrong thing. Later, Lamb can do things, and so can the Wolf, through it. By the end of the year Lamb has keys, and accounts, and a purse.

## What the farm has that Nan's lamb did not

In the fable the lamb has only arguments, and arguments are not enough. At Big Field the lamb has neighbours.

It has people who can check its work, rules that can be written into the gate rather than left to good intentions, and defenses that can be switched on, measured and improved. None of them is perfect. Some of them will miss, and the labs show you exactly where. But each one is a reason the Wolf has not thought of yet.

Nan's version of the story ended at the stream. This one is not finished.
